## Crafting is five times faster

The standard rule gives you 5 gp of progress per day of downtime, which makes a 50 gp potion a ten day job. The DM's ruling:

> we could speed that to say, 25g per day of downtime. So you would need to be living in a town or city for 2 days, and spend half of its cost on raw materials.

> 1 healing potion = 2 days of downtime + 25g

So: **25 gp of progress per day**, you must be staying in a town or city, and raw materials cost half the item's market value.

## Camera on earns inspiration

> If you have your camera on, you will get an inspiration point for that session (D20 re-roll)

## Roleplay is turn-based

The DM directs conversation round the table rather than letting it free-for-all, so quieter players get the same airtime. His own example:

> GM: NPC says such and such, Lucian, how do you respond?
> Lucian: I draw my blade and say ...
> GM: Mundus, you see this happening across the room. Do you react?

This came out of a session-zero style discussion where Tobias Goreguts said the hard part was knowing when to listen and when to ask questions, and that he didn't want to take the spotlight from anyone.

## Weapon masteries

Melee characters get weapon mastery properties from level 1. You know **two at a time**, and can swap one by practising with a different weapon during a long rest.

## Feats

A feat at level 1, and another every few levels. The level 1 list The DM offered was: Alert, Crafter, Healer, Lucky, Magic Initiate, Musician, Savage Attacker, Skilled, Tavern Brawler, Tough.

The full text of each is on D&D Beyond, which everyone has through The DM's content sharing.

## Scheduling

Sessions aim for 7:30 to 8pm Pacific, balancing people finishing work on the west coast against mountain-time players wanting an early night.
