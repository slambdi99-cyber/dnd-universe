Mining at Copper Ridge has torn open sulfide-rich seams, and blasting in the Dire Foothills has fractured the watershed. Groundwater now bleeds into the mines instead of the plains, leaving Copper Vale to dry out.

The abundance of exposed sulfide-rich copper produces highly acidic runoff, etching vivid streaks of green patina along the ruddy mountain cliffs of the Verdigris Teeth. With fresh water dwindling, the remaining lowlands stagnate, giving rise to the Shallow Bog.

The through-line of the region is water: who has it, who took it, and what is left living in what remains.
